Industrial Salt Storage Buildings

Why Do You Need Salt Storage?

To explain why you need salt storage building solutions, let’s first talk about salt’s value. Salt is an essential nutrient and food preservative. Plus, it makes foods taste better. Besides being for food, however, salt is also used for water softening, animal nutrition, and making certain chemical compounds such as chlorine.

Also, it works as a de-icer. In Tennessee and Alabama, ice and snow are rarer, but when it snows, it loves to make an impression. Storing ready-to-use road salt provides an inexpensive, non-toxic method to de-ice.

While you may not picture salt when you think of perishable goods, improperly stored salt can lead to lumps, which can be problematic when spreading salt. In addition, a salt storage building can prevent salt from possibly contaminating streams or dissolving.

When it comes to sand and salt storage, salt is best stored under cover and away from the elements. When you store salt properly, you don’t need to spend money on crushing salt to eliminate lumps or possibly lose money due to it dissolving from the rain.

Bulk Salt Storage

Regarding bulk materials, bulk salt is valuable during a winter storm. Salt does not have an expiration date as long as it’s stored properly, as it’s a natural resource that has been around since the beginning of Earth. Bulk salt storage means you don’t have to worry about emergency shipments during a winter storm, which can be delayed due to the weather.

Salt Storage Building Benefits

Protects the Salt From Direct Exposure to the Elements

Salt may clump together if stored in a humid area, leading to difficulties unclumping it when it’s ready to be used. In addition, salt can dissolve if exposed to the rain. Salt storage buildings prevent salt from being exposed to these elements.

Building Strength and Durable Fabric Can Withstand Significant Wind and Snowfall

A sand and salt storage building prevents your supply from being lost due to a windstorm or heavy snowfall.

Minimizes Product Loss From Wetting and Leaching

Salt and sand storage buildings prevent your supply from being lost, which can lead to lost profits and an inability to prepare during a winter storm.

Ability to Maintain the Salt Condition Without Clumping or Heavy Crusting

Salt and sand storage buildings can keep your salt as grainy as it was when you first imported it.

Protecting Groundwater and Wetlands From Runoff

Improperly stored salt can run into groundwater and wetlands, causing contamination. A salt and sand storage facility prevents this.

Increased Employee Safety

Hot Dip Galvanized to Prevent Corrosion

Fabric Liners Keep the Steel Out of Contact With Salt

Maximized Height, Clearance, and Square Footage for the Price

Flexibility to Load From a Portable Conveyor System or by Dumping and Pushing

Variety of Foundations to Suit Your Budget

Selecting the Site for Salt Storage Structures

Make It Safe

The ideal salt storage building allows heavy equipment to enter with visibility in all directions while away from frequently traveled roads. In addition, the site needs protection from unauthorized personnel, such as fences.

Keep It Accessible

The storage area should allow front-end loaders enough space to move without any doors interfering. Even if there are storms, these buildings should allow for easy access.

Keep It Legal

Your building should comply with local and state zoning requirements and building regulations.

Keep It Tidy

You want a building with no junk or scrap material that could interfere with the salt or the vehicles. In addition, you want an establishment where it’s easy to remove any excess salt or trash.

Keep It Economical

You want protected rock salt piles that won’t waste any money or harm the environment and your storage facility to be in a convenient location.

Control And/or Collect All Drainage

Your salt storage building should have drainage away from stockpiles, with nearby support from pads or aprons. Drainage should not lead into any reservoirs or groundwater supplies. In addition, you must contact local laws when disposing of brine.

A salt storage building can be economical, but it can vary depending on the amount of salt you need to store and if there are any other functions. The best way to know how much a building will cost, however, is if you have an idea of how many tons you need to be stored. An honest subcontractor can give you an accurate estimate regarding costs.

Design Factors for Salt Storage Buildings

Load Requirements

To ensure safety and insurability, your building must follow International Building Codes (IBC).

 

Capacity Requirements

Knowing how dense your salt is can help determine the best width and length for your storage building.

 

Reducing the Risk of Corrosive Damage

Salt is among the many corrosive materials, so your building must be hot-dip galvanized. This will protect your building from any corroding from loose salt.

 

Ventilation

Proper ventilation will promote air movement and ensure your building remains dry, preventing clumping.

 

Consider Independent Foundations and Panels

Your salt storage building may utilize independent foundations and pre-cast walls, which can be more economical and help you to reduce corrosion. Of course, your salt and sand storage solution can depend on your unique needs.
