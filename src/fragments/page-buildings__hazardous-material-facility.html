What is Hazardous Waste?

Many processes of our modern world produce waste, and some of it is dangerous or harmful to the health of people or the environment. Hazardous materials can be liquids, solids, gases, or sludgy semi-liquids. Hazardous waste can be as common as lead-based coatings, or as complicated sounding as polychlorinated biphenyls.

It can be complicated to discern if the waste being generated is actually hazardous waste according to the regulatory definition. The EPA has many documents to help with that process and also uses a fairly basic flow chart to help generators determine the classification of their waste materials

What is a Hazardous Waste Facility Building?

When a building is designated for hazardous waste, it is meant to be a disposal facility for things like compressed gas cylinders, treated wood, chemical residue, and asbestos removed from demolition activities in older buildings.

The construction requirements for these facilities are strenuous…even more so than the requirements for entities that generate or transport hazardous waste.

Facilities for the disposal of waste materials are necessary for the safe processing of hazardous debris. Specific requirements vary from state to state and also depend upon the type of waste being handled.

Federal Regulations When Disposing of Hazardous Materials

The EPA, the Environmental Protection Agency, and the RCRA, the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act, regulate everything about hazardous materials from the time they are generated all the way through disposal or storage. Any facility that deals with hazardous wastes must comply with federal regulations and implement all actions required.

Since hazardous materials are products that can put humans and the environment at risk, transporting them to facilities to be disposed of is a carefully controlled process. The term for the materials is often shortened to HAZMAT. It is seen on road signs, and vehicles, and is shortened to HM in government regulations.

Not knowing the regulations doesn’t offer any excuse for failure to fulfill them! Proper documentation of every step of the process and frequent inspection to ensure accordance with the regulations are all part of the system.

What is a Hazardous Waste Disposal Facility?

Across the country, there are facilities that treat, store, or dispose of hazardous waste materials. There are many regulations and much oversight of these facilities. They are often referred to TSDFs….treatment, storage, and disposal facilities.

When is Hazardous Material Facility Building Required?

It is often safe and effective compliance to store hazardous waste in a primary facility, but when certain levels of quantity are reached, then they must be stored in specially designed storage buildings intended to be a site specifically for hazardous waste. Any hazardous substance that doesn’t fit in a hazmat storage container, requires a designated building.

One interesting need of a TSDF is to house hazardous waste until it is ready to be placed in a landfill. The waste must be in a lined container to prepare it for being placed in soil to immobilize the hazardous constituents it contains.

The Risks of Improperly Disposing of Hazardous Wastes

In the United States, there are nearly 42 million tons of hazardous wastes generated yearly, from industries, businesses, and households. There are approximately 25 incidents a year where hazardous substances are released. When this happens, there is a risk to the safety of people and the environment. There is also a risk to the workers who are employed to clean up the incident.

Improperly handled hazardous waste can contaminate soil and water sources. These wastes can also increase fire hazard, since most of them are flammable.
